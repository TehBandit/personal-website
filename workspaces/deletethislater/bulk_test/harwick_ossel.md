HARWICK OSSEL

Harwick Ossel is a Senior Surveyor of the Admiralty and currently serves as the deputy head of the Cartographic Division. He is fifty-three, heavyset, with a deliberate manner that reads as gravitas to those who admire him and as obstruction to those who have worked against him.

He came up through the navigational corps the conventional way — thirty years of service, careful alliances, and a talent for being indispensable to whoever was making decisions at any given time. He does not appear to have exceptional aptitude for navigation itself. His skill is organizational. He knows where things are filed, who controls access to what, and which requests can be delayed indefinitely without formal objection.

The complaint filed against him by Rella Corse alleged that he had been supplying altered survey data to a private concern — believed to be a consortium operating in the coastal lowlands — that had an interest in keeping Admiralty patrol routes predictable and away from certain anchorages. Ossel denied this. The investigation found no corroborating evidence, which may reflect genuine innocence or may reflect Ossel's familiarity with how Admiralty documentation works.

He was promoted to his current position eight months after Corse's dismissal.

Ossel lives in the upper district of Tiveck in rented accommodation considerably nicer than a deputy surveyor's salary would normally support. He is a member of two Admiralty social committees and is regarded as a capable administrator. He has no publicly known enemies, which some observers find more suspicious than a long list would be.

There is a standing rumor — unverified — that he has a private arrangement with someone in the Deepwater Guild, through which Admiralty survey data is made available to certain parties in advance of its official release. If true, this would constitute a significant breach of the Compact of Survey Rights signed between the Admiralty and the Guild forty years ago.
