RELLA CORSE

Rella Corse spent eleven years as a Navigator First Class in the Admiralty before her dismissal. The official record lists the cause as "conduct inconsistent with the duties of the navigational corps," a phrase sufficiently vague to mean almost anything. Those who served with her suggest the real issue was a formal complaint she filed against a senior officer named Harwick Ossel, who she claimed had been falsifying survey coordinates to steer Admiralty vessels away from routes controlled by his private interests. The complaint was investigated and closed without findings. Ossel was promoted. Corse was dismissed.

She spent the following two years doing freelance chart correction work for independent merchants in the harbor district, living in a rented room above a fish saltery in Salt Warren. It was during this period that she met Dovan Strake. The precise nature of that introduction is unknown.

Corse is in her early forties, lean, with the deliberate movements of someone who learned to work in close quarters aboard ships. She keeps her hair cut short. She is known to carry a brass sighting instrument — a compact surveying tool — not as a weapon but as a habit. Old navigators tend to keep something in their hands.

Her current residence is a rented storehouse at the edge of the 14th Lowland, which she shares with no one. She is formal with people she has just met and relaxed with almost no one. The exception, reportedly, is Dovan Strake, with whom she has had some variety of long-running argument that those who have overheard it cannot fully characterize as either antagonism or friendship.

She has not filed any document with the Admiralty since her dismissal. She does not speak about Harwick Ossel. She has, on at least two occasions, been seen in the vicinity of the archive corridor at the Deepwater Guild's lower hall, though she is not known to have entered.
