THE TIDE COMPACT

The Tide Compact was formed twelve years ago on the salt flats north of Tiveck, when three rival smuggling consortiums agreed to stop killing each other long enough to turn a profit. Its founding members were Rella Corse, a former Navigator of the Admiralty who had been quietly dismissed for reasons that remain sealed; Dovan Strake, a fence operating out of a chandlery in Salt Warren; and an unnamed broker known only as the Inkhand, whose identity has never been confirmed.

The Compact's primary business is the movement of goods that the Admiralty classifies as contraband — preserved meats during the rationing periods, salvaged navigational instruments, and, more recently, documents whose provenance the guilds would prefer to contest in court rather than the streets.

Rella Corse runs the logistics side from a leased storehouse at the edge of the 14th Lowland, using the warehouse's legitimate salt-drying contracts as cover. She is meticulous, rarely speaks above a murmur, and has a reputation for cutting loose anyone who draws attention to the operation. There are at least three former associates of the Compact who simply stopped appearing in the harbor district without explanation.

Dovan Strake handles buyers. He is affable, loud, and precisely the kind of person you don't expect to be laundering the proceeds of document theft. He drinks at the Wrecked Compass three nights a week and is, by all accounts, genuinely well-liked.

The Inkhand's role is unclear. Some accounts suggest the Inkhand is the Compact's connection to someone inside the Deepwater Guild — perhaps a scribe or an archivist with access to records that should not be accessible. Others believe the Inkhand is merely a story the other two invented to suggest the Compact has more reach than it does.

The Compact does not directly compete with the Deepwater Guild. Whether this is because they have an arrangement or because they are simply too small to be worth crushing is a matter of ongoing debate among those who track such things.
