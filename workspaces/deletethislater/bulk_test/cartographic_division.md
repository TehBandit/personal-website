THE CARTOGRAPHIC DIVISION

The Cartographic Division is the branch of the Admiralty responsible for maintaining all official navigational charts, survey records, and coastal data for the region. It operates out of the upper two floors of the Admiralty's main building in Tiveck, and its archive occupies a separate, humidity-controlled vault in the basement of the same structure.

The Division employs approximately forty-five people: fourteen active surveyors, twelve chart drafters, eight archivists, and eleven administrative staff. It is headed, nominally, by Chief Surveyor Vane — an elderly man who has largely delegated operational responsibility to his deputy, Harwick Ossel. Vane comes to the office three mornings a week and leaves before midday.

The Division's records are considered authoritative. Any dispute about coastal rights, territorial claims, or navigational precedent is eventually resolved by reference to the Cartographic Division's files. This makes access to those files — particularly the ability to alter or delay their contents — a significant form of institutional power.

The archive is accessible to Admiralty personnel by request, to Guild representatives under a formal access agreement, and to third parties only by explicit authorization from the Chief Surveyor or his deputy. Unauthorized access is treated as a criminal matter.

There have been two documented incidents in the past decade where survey data released by the Division was found to diverge from independent measurements taken by merchant vessels. In both cases the discrepancy was attributed to instrument calibration error. In both cases the affected routes were subsequently deprioritized for Admiralty patrol.

The relationship between the Cartographic Division and the Deepwater Guild is formally cooperative and informally complex. The Guild has, on several occasions, challenged the Division's survey data in situations where the official charts would have reduced the Guild's operational reach. These disputes are usually resolved quietly.
